# Research Framework

## Research question

How can predictive analytics and business intelligence improve healthcare operational decision-making while maintaining transparency, responsible AI and a strict non-clinical scope?

## Constructs

- analytics capability
- operational visibility
- demand and capacity alignment
- patient-flow efficiency
- service experience
- explainability
- human oversight
- decision-support quality
- digital transformation maturity

## Operational outcomes

- appointment utilization
- no-show and cancellation rates
- average, median and P90 waiting time
- target-wait exceedance
- department throughput
- capacity utilization
- staffing pressure
- satisfaction and recommendation scores

## Predictive tasks

1. No-show classification
2. Cancellation classification
3. Waiting-time risk classification
4. Department capacity-pressure classification
5. Department and appointment-type demand forecasting

## Validation strategy

- chronologically ordered data
- expanding temporal cross-validation
- later temporal holdout
- class-imbalance-aware PR-AUC
- discrimination and calibration metrics
- forecasting baselines
- uncertainty-interval coverage
- fairness monitoring on excluded demographic groups
- end-to-end consistency auditing

## Interpretation rules

Prediction is not causation. Operational suggestions are generated only when transparent evidence and limitations can be shown. No model output is interpreted clinically.

## Future research

- external validation using lawfully governed de-identified data
- hierarchical probabilistic demand forecasting
- constrained optimization for appointment slots
- causal evaluation of reminder and scheduling interventions
- uncertainty-aware capacity planning
- participatory fairness and human-factors evaluation
